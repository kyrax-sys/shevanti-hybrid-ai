1. XGBOOST WINS ON RMSE, NOT ON MAE — AND THAT DIFFERENCE MATTERS
XGBoost has the LOWEST RMSE of all three models (135.80, vs 138.26 for naive
and 169.47 for the average). RMSE penalises large misses more heavily than
MAE does, so this means XGBoost is the most reliable model specifically on
the days where the price jumps or drops sharply — the "difficult days" the
project guide asks us to evaluate separately (Stage 8).

However, on MAE, the naive baseline is actually BEST (90.25 vs XGBoost's
104.00). This is not a bug — it's a real property of this price series.
Looking at the raw data, this market has long flat stretches where the same
price repeats for several days in a row (roughly 20-40% of days show zero
change year to year). On those ordinary days, "tomorrow = today" is very
hard to beat, and XGBoost's added complexity introduces small extra error
where a flat guess would have been exactly right.

TAKEAWAY: XGBoost is not "worse than the baseline" in a simple sense — it
trades a bit of everyday accuracy for much better behaviour on the days that
actually matter for the project's central question (does the hybrid help
during large price movements?). This needs to be reported honestly, not
hidden — the project guide explicitly says a model that loses to a simple
rule is still a valid, reportable finding.

2. DIRECTIONAL ACCURACY: NAIVE'S LOW SCORE IS A DEFINITION ARTIFACT
Naive scored worst on directional accuracy (28.5%). This is expected, not a
weakness of "naive forecasting" in general — because the naive model always
predicts "no change," it can only be counted correct on days where the
actual price also didn't move. Its DirAcc score is really just measuring how
often the market was flat (~28% of test days), not how well it "predicts
direction."
The 7-day average actually has the BEST directional accuracy (46.6%),
slightly ahead of XGBoost (43.3%). This is worth flagging as an open
question for Stage 8 rather than a settled conclusion — XGBoost does not
yet clearly beat a simple average on direction, and this is exactly the
kind of gap a hybrid model should try to close.

3. FEATURE IMPORTANCE — WHAT XGBOOST ACTUALLY RELIES ON
lag_1              0.371   <- by far the strongest single feature

trend_7            0.021
lag_7              0.019
lag_2              0.018
lag_3              0.016

Yesterday's price (lag_1) alone accounts for over a third of the model's
decisions. After that, the recent 7-day range (rolling_max_7, rolling_min_7)
matters much more than the recent average or trend. This fits the shape of
the data: prices tend to sit in "bands" (e.g. clustering around 210, 420,
820) and jump between them rather than drifting smoothly, so knowing the
recent high/low tells the model which band the market is currently in.
Longer-window features (rolling_mean_14, trend_7) barely matter -- 14 days
of history adds little beyond what the last 7 days already say.
NOTE (per project rules): this shows what XGBoost relied on statistically.
It is not a causal claim about why the market price moved.

4. ACTUAL VS PREDICTED GRAPH (2016 test set)
XGBoost tracks the general shape of the price series reasonably well and
follows most of the mid-range fluctuations closely. Its clearest weakness
is at sharp step-changes -- moments where the actual price jumps almost
vertically between price bands (e.g. straight from ~400 to 820). At these
points the forecast visibly lags for a day or two before catching up, since
the model is leaning heavily on lag_1 and the recent 7-day range, both of
which only "know" about the jump after it has already happened.
This is the single clearest opportunity for the hybrid model: XGBoost is
structurally a day behind at level-shifts, and a statistical model with a
faster-reacting error-correction term (ARIMA residuals) could help close
that gap, if it reacts faster to the shift.

5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE HYBRID (residual learning)
- XGBoost's biggest weakness is the first 1-2 days after a sharp level
  shift, not general noise. This is the specific failure mode the hybrid
  (ARIMA + XGBoost residual correction) should target.
- lag_1 and recent min/max dominate: if ARIMA's own forecast is fed into
  the hybrid, it should be tested alongside lag_1 rather than replacing it
  -- both may carry overlapping but not identical information.
- Comparison against the statistical (ARIMA) model is NOT included in this
  file. That comparison can only be made fairly once Sujan's rolling
  one-step ARIMA predictions are in results/tables/ and merged with mine
  on date, using the same evaluate() function (see shared_utils.py). Do
  not claim "ML beats statistical" or vice versa until that merge is done.
